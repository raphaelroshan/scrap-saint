**Scrap Saint: the manifestation of freely given repair**

15 September 2026. The user's chosen foundation is a devastated machine world, spreading corruption, and a Saint manifested through repairs given freely. This supersedes the secular origin and human-evacuation foundation recommended in world_and_relic_direction.md. The elaborations below are proposed expressions of that foundation. No runtime mechanics change.

The world is wounded. Across its ruined districts, corruption turns once-useful machines against the living. Yet some still give what little they have to mend another: a length of wire, a working joint, the last clean measure of oil. Those acts leave something behind. Over generations, that freely given care gathers in discarded parts until, in a ruined workshop, it takes a body and wakes.

The Scrap Saint is the manifestation of that care. Its body is small because most of the mercies that made it were small.

**What changes from the previous proposal**

The religious undertone is real within the fiction. Repair can be an act of grace, and the Saint's existence is a miracle. Individual characters can disagree about what that miracle means, but the story should not eventually explain it away as a planned invention or a misunderstood maintenance routine.

The world belongs to machines with their own histories, beliefs and relationships. Human departure is not the foundation. Retain lived-in workshops, particular trades, warm communities and creatures with recognisable physical habits. Place them within devastation and spiritual danger.

The First Engine remains the destination of the pilgrimage and a source of conflicting religious claims. Some believe it can heal the world. Others believe the wound began there. Its actual relationship to the catastrophe remains an authored mystery; the Saint was not secretly designed by it.

**The manifestation**

No single craftsman built the Saint. Its components passed through many bodies and many acts of service. A bracket held a stranger upright. A bell guided lost travellers. A lens was given to someone who could no longer see their work. Eventually these parts reached the same place.

Proposed opening: in an abandoned workshop, a repairer uses their last sound fastener to mend a machine they do not expect to see again. Across the room, a discarded bell sounds without a clapper. Old seams catch a faint light. Parts begin, awkwardly, to hold together. The Saint opens its eye.

That final repair gives the accumulated grace a moment to manifest; it does not make one repairer the sole creator. The body contains incompatible parts because kindness was never issued to a common specification.

The Saint has no complete doctrine and no inherited certainty. It recognises hurt before it understands holiness. Its story concerns what to do with the care that made it, rather than recovering a lost factory identity. Keep its inspection tilts, unreliable joints and solemn treatment of small objects. Being sacred need not make it grand or infallible.

**Devastation and corruption**

The original catastrophe broke the connected world. The corruption spreads through what remains. Ruin and corruption are distinct: a broken, rusted or improvised machine is not morally tainted simply because its body is damaged.

Working name for the corruption: the Hollowing. It consumes the relationship between a machine's purpose and the life that purpose served. What remains is an appetite or command with no room for another being's needs. Its ultimate cause should remain open until the larger story is authored.

This is more than a software error. Proposed signs include cavities where an inner light should be, movement continuing through impossible breaks, and a familiar signal answered by something inside the casing. Use these selectively. Ordinary rust and the Saint's mismatched repairs must remain visually distinct from corruption.

| Creature | Useful origin retained | Corrupted expression |
|---|---|---|
| Rivet Hound | Pursues loose fittings and moving faults. | It experiences another body's freedom as a defect to be pinned down. Its existing charge becomes an act of compulsive fastening. |
| Scrap Mite | Gathers usable material. | Hunger has outgrown sorting; every living assembly looks dismantlable. Preserve harmless scavenger life elsewhere so the entire ecology is not condemned. |
| Choir Drone | Carries signals that help machines act together. | Shared rhythm becomes enforced obedience. Its voice has lost the pauses in which another voice could answer. |
| Rust Pilgrim | Travels with repair tools. | It compulsively keeps corrupted machinery functioning. Its excellent repairs prolong suffering it can no longer recognise. |
| Forklift Brute | Clears space and carries heavy loads. | It recognises bodies only as obstructions. Its weight and shove communicate that disregard. |
| Cinder Spitter | Releases dangerous furnace residue. | It cannot distinguish relief from spreading the wound; each discharge deposits danger somewhere else. |

These are proposed lore interpretations of existing roles, not new combat requirements. Enemies remain readable through ordinary movement and automatic attacks. Do not turn every creature into a puzzle requiring a particular sacred weapon.

Some machinery is dangerous without being corrupted. Some conscious enemies knowingly serve an institution. Keep those distinctions so every conflict does not have the same explanation.

**Relics and miracles**

Matter retains an impression of freely given repair. A relic's practical history explains its physical operation; the grace carried through that history explains why, in the Saint's hands, it can exceed its original purpose. A rivet gun should still recoil like a rivet gun when it performs a miracle.

| Relic | Sacred history to develop | Proposed flavour line |
|---|---|---|
| Nailer of Small Mercies | Passed between repairers who mended homes and bodies unable to repay them. | “No account was kept of what it held together.” |
| Bell of the Last Shift | Rung so the exhausted could stop working, even when their overseers would not release them. | “Someone rang it before permission came.” |
| Penance Winch | Once used to recover valuable equipment; later lowered into the same shafts for those considered beyond recovery. | “It was lowered again after the search was called off.” |
| Cable of Contrition | A line repeatedly repaired by those who had relied on it, then left for the next traveller. | “Every splice is a hand that did not let go.” |
| Candle-Nailer | A recovery tool carrying lights left beside bodies whose names were unknown. | “A light was left for them anyway.” |
| Hymn Coil | Its windings preserve fragments of signals once used to answer isolated machines. | “There is a reply beneath the static.” |
| Altar Mortar | A community font rebuilt into a weapon when the sanctuary around it could no longer shelter the living. | “The sanctuary travels with what remains.” |
| Procession Gear | A service mechanism that carried the weight of bodies too damaged to travel alone. | “Another place in the procession.” |
| Mercy Rail | The Nailer transformed as the care within its repairs finds a greater physical expression. | “What held one broken thing together now carries farther.” |

These lines are candidates, not replacement tooltips. The plain description must still explain the actual attack. Saint's Rivet can carry the same sacred logic: a fastening given at cost, preserving the memory of a repair made without a claim over the recipient.

Keep some mundane relic histories and dry workshop humour from the earlier proposal. A tool can carry grace and still have a manufacturer advising against its current use. If every item speaks in solemn absolutes, holiness becomes background noise.

**Faith in daily life**

Blessings are living interpretations of repair and responsibility. Workshop Gospel teaches that care is work performed with attention. Bell Ward teaches the duty to warn and answer. The Procession carries those who cannot continue alone. The Mourner insists that care includes recognising when a body cannot be restored.

Institutions may disagree about the Saint. A workshop calls it a blessing. A keeper of doctrine wants it examined. A damaged pilgrim simply asks whether it can help. Avoid making every religious figure foolish or every organised faith corrupt. Real devotion exists throughout the world and helped bring the Saint into being.

Freely given repair means care that does not demand ownership, obedience or repayment from its recipient. Fair exchange and paid craftsmanship still have a place. Ada can charge for scarce parts without contradicting the premise. The relic shop remains a practical workshop within the pilgrimage.

**How this supports play**

The Saint's manifestation is its origin, not a repair quota the player must maintain. Skipping optional repairs does not drain holiness, cancel powers or label the player unworthy. Field repair pickups and optional map work preserve the established roaming game.

Combat can break a corrupted body or end an immediate threat. It does not automatically restore the person who may once have occupied it. Keep salvation, recovery and final loss available as different authored outcomes; do not attach a rescue obligation to every ordinary defeat.

Weapon evolution should reveal both physical reconstruction and sacred force. The result remains readable: the weapon braces, discharges, makes contact and leaves an understandable aftermath. Mercy Rail needs satisfying weight against the ordinary roster, not an encounter designed to certify its holiness.

The Saint may discover that repairing the world requires decisions about what should continue, what must change and what can finally rest. Preserve those questions around the First Engine. Do not reduce the conclusion to making all broken things obey their old instructions again.
