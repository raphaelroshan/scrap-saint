**Scrap Saint: world, creature and relic design**

**Superseded foundation:** the user selected a devastated machine world with spreading corruption, where freely given repairs cause the Saint to manifest. See [the sacred origin revision](saint_of_freely_given_repairs.md). The secular origin, human-evacuation foundation and interpretation of sainthood as merely a nickname below are historical alternatives. Practical histories and tactile presentation remain useful where compatible with the new premise.

15 September 2026. An evaluated creative proposal, with a recommended direction. New lore and presentation below are authored concepts, not claims about implemented content. This document preserves the existing names and industrial devotional premise while explicitly proposing changes to the old origin reveal and campaign framing.

**Recommendation: a pilgrimage through the work that outlived its purpose.**

The Works once kept a valley warm, supplied its water and carried its people home. The people evacuated generations ago. Their machines remain. Some have made lives together. Others still carry out instructions that no longer fit the world.

The Saint is a small travelling repair machine assembled from several incompatible trades. It wakes with an instruction to restore the First Engine. Along the road, it discovers that the machines living here need more than a return to the old schedule.

Combat is the loud, physical expression of that premise: a little improvised body making extraordinary use of workaday equipment. Quiet moments reveal who used these objects, who maintains them now, and what they might become.

**What needs improving in the existing design**

The existing bible has a strong tone, an appealing player silhouette and several good relic names. The weak point is the connection between them. Industrial districts, sacred weapons and hostile machines sometimes read as separate lists. Too many explanations end at a damaged instruction or an unanswered god. The First Engine's deliberate creation of a uniquely free Saint also explains away the most interesting part of its identity: being assembled incorrectly.

The revision should give each creature an observable habit and each relic a former use. Communities should make the world worth visiting. The Saint's identity should emerge through its relationships and build choices. These improvements can reach the game through animation and short text before any campaign system is needed.

Current gameplay remains the foundation: free movement, automatic weapons, optional repairs and field recovery. Mercy Rail remains optional. Creature lore must not become a justification for mandatory counter items, relay defence or manual interruption sequences.

Choose A as the foundation. Use C to give the abandoned districts new life. Use a little of D for social customs and individual characters. Keep B as a rejected full setting; large machinery can still feel awe-inspiring without making the entire map a god's corpse. This is a hierarchy of ideas, not four competing explanations of the world.

**The recommended world, made specific**

The Works is the everyday name for the connected settlements, yards and service districts around the First Engine. Avoid another grand proper noun for the planet. The story concerns this valley and the people who live here now.

The evacuation is recorded history. Severe floods and failing hillside works made continued human habitation untenable. The First Engine coordinated an evacuation and kept essential systems running for those expected to return. No return order came. The game need not turn human disappearance into its central mystery or promise the discovery of the last human.

The Engine still distributes intermittent power and work instructions. It was built to reconcile demands across the valley, but its old records no longer describe the present. A pipe recorded as abandoned now supplies a machine settlement. A condemned yard contains a garden. Local acts of survival keep contradicting the plan it is trying to complete.

Its power is substantial and limited. It can schedule work, recognise registered equipment and operate connected machinery. It cannot see every settlement, control every individual or predict the Saint's choices. That limitation gives travel and local knowledge a purpose.

The people of the Works are machines with lives beyond their original tasks. Personhood is established through relationships, memory and choice, not a humanoid silhouette. They trade repaired components, argue over shared heat, teach apprentices and keep unreliable friends. Some have invented hobbies their makers never anticipated.

Machines call an act a miracle when it makes life possible where the official plan said it could not be done. The practice is sincere. A working lamp can deserve gratitude without requiring a cosmology lesson. Leave room for wonder: characters may disagree about whether a familiar sound in a rebuilt relic is a recording, a memory or someone answering.

Five recurring customs give the setting texture:

- At a shared charging bench, the warmest connector is left for whoever has travelled furthest.
- A machine's old job plate is kept after it changes occupation. The new name is painted beside it.
- The last bolt removed from an irreparable body is kept until its friends decide where it belongs.
- A repaired public bell is tested softly first, so it does not wake the night workers.
- In some settlements, a day off is observed by carrying tools somewhere they are not needed.

Not every object needs grief attached to it. Include worn games, bad repairs that became beloved, proud workmanship and harmless inconveniences. These details make the losses matter.

**Three versions of the Saint**

| Origin | Emotional promise | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| The Engine's intended exception | The First Engine secretly designed a machine capable of choosing differently. | Gives the plot a clean twist, but makes freedom part of the creator's plan and reduces the significance of ordinary assembly. Retire this reveal in the recommended revision. |
| The communal repair | Several ordinary machines kept repairing a damaged service body with whatever they could spare. No one designed the resulting whole. | Best fit. Its body is evidence that others believed something unfinished was worth keeping. |
| The borrowed person | A reconstruction carries fragments of a dead worker's memories and searches for that person's identity. | Strong mystery, but risks making its current self a lesser copy and making every relic a clue to one lost owner. |

Choose the communal repair. The Saint's bell, waterworks arm, archive lens and protective shell retain their existing provenance. Their incompatibility is practical: different tolerances, different habits, different ideas about what counts as urgent. Its original service instruction to restore the First Engine survived because it was stamped into a protected task plate.

The Saint begins by asking which workshop made it. Eventually it learns that the better question is who kept it going. There is no single maker to reveal. One repair was skilled; another was desperate; a third was performed by someone who insists it was only temporary. Their choices were ordinary acts with an extraordinary cumulative result.

It is called Saint by others after a small act of help. The player does not need to complete a mandatory repair tutorial to earn the name. The opening can establish an earlier, off-screen kindness: Ada remembers the machine holding a lamp steady through a long repair. The title is a community nickname that gradually gathers weight.

Personality should be readable without constant dialogue. It turns its whole body to inspect a damaged fitting. Its new arm tries to fold into an old resting position and fails. After an evolution, it briefly checks whether it still fits through a doorway. These are presentation proposals; they must never delay movement.

Sample voice:

> Ada: “That bracket was temporary.”
>
> Saint: “It has been very dependable.”

> Saint, finding its old inspection label: “Was I safe?”
>
> Ada: “You were getting there.”

The Saint should sometimes act without consulting an old instruction. Save those moments for meaningful choices; a steady stream of literal-minded robot jokes would flatten its personality.

**Creature lore that produces behaviour**

Three approaches were considered. A universal corruption explains everything cheaply but gives the roster one motive. Fully personified enemy factions create richer politics but make repeated crowd combat morally cumbersome. The recommended majority is feral working equipment: recognisable operations persisting in changed conditions, mixed with a smaller number of deliberate institutional opponents.

Hostile machines are not all secretly friendly people waiting for one repair button. Some have little capacity beyond a dangerous task. Others choose to enforce an order. Ordinary combat disables active bodies and leaves recoverable material; it does not guarantee that every defeated machine survives. The Mourner tradition exists partly because some losses are final. Avoid presenting hundreds of obligatory kills as hundreds of individual rescue failures.

Each creature needs a former purpose, a present motive, a readable body and one behaviour. The visual cues below are proposed dressing for existing roles, not additional required mechanics. Runtime notes remain authoritative where content descriptions are aspirational.

| Creature | Former purpose and present motive | Character expressed through action | Short bestiary line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scrap Mite | Small sorting crawlers gather loose fasteners. Several generations of improvised rebuilding have made them opportunistic scavengers. | A crowded, low silhouette; little clamp mouths and a bright cluster of eyes. Their swarm feels like a workshop drawer spilled into motion. | “They sort by taste. The brass goes first.” |
| Rivet Hound | Mobile seam inspectors ran beside moving carriages and pinned loose panels. They now register the Saint's rattling body as work that is getting away. | An inspection lens fixes, forelegs brace and the body lunges. Preserve its ordinary charge role. | “It has found a fault. Please stop running with it.” |
| Choir Drone | Acoustic timing devices helped crews coordinate in smoke and darkness. Their surviving cadence conflicts with modern improvised machines. | Speaker shutters open before the support or suppression field appears; their movement briefly steadies while sounding. | “Everyone on the same note. Everyone on the same shift.” |
| Rust Pilgrim | A travelling maintenance cart still follows its last service route, repairing machines it recognises as assigned equipment. | Protective posture, dangling service tags and a visible repair connection explain its support role. | “The route is obsolete. The repairs are excellent.” |
| Forklift Brute | A cargo mover clears marked access lanes. An unregistered assembly moving through them is classified as misplaced load. | Forks lower, weight settles and it accelerates into a shove. A delivery tag remains stuck to its chassis. | “Destination amended: elsewhere.” |
| Cinder Spitter | A mobile slag-disposal nozzle ejects residue into marked bins. Missing bins have not ended the disposal schedule. | A hot throat and a sputtering pressure build precede its delayed ground blast. | “Stand clear of the collection point.” |
| Memory Crane | A training and salvage rig reproduces the motions of equipment under assessment. It recognises components of the Saint through old service records. | An inspection pose precedes a copied attack silhouette. Use only the supported copy behaviours; no bespoke weapon-counter requirement. | “It remembers the correct way to lift that arm.” |
| Foreman Engine | A yard supervisor is still completing a clearance order issued before evacuation. The occupied workshop remains on its demolition list. | Physical site markings and heavy equipment movements explain its existing hazard scheduling. | “Work cannot finish while someone is still using the workshop.” |

To expand the ecology later, begin with ordinary material cycles rather than extra combat roles. Three non-enabled sketches demonstrate the range:

| Candidate | Life in the Works | Why it is useful to the setting |
|---|---|---|
| Washer Nest | Small scavengers arrange smooth rings around a warm pipe. | Shows habitat and preference. It can be background dressing without becoming a new enemy or collectible system. |
| Night Cart | An old transport follows its route after dark, carrying anything left politely at a stop. | Creates a relationship between settlements and machinery; offers warmth without dialogue. |
| Rain Gauge Keeper | A narrow tower machine records weather on a roll of paper and shelters small repairers beneath its awning. | Makes maintenance civic and social. It need not offer a quest. |

Neither all wildlife nor every large silhouette should be hostile. Keep harmless life away from targetable enemy visual language so the player can read combat without hesitation.

**Relics: histories that remain visible when they fire**

Three foundations were considered: fragments of divine machinery, improvised combat scrap, and tools with remembered service. Divine fragments offer grandeur but weaken the humble-object fantasy. Combat scrap is immediately legible but risks generic weapons with industrial decoration. Tools with remembered service best connect the world's warmth to physical action.

A relic gets four pieces of authorship: its old job, a particular sign of use, how the Saint operates it now, and one short line of flavour. The tool need not come from a famous owner. Practical detail carries more weight than another legendary battle.

| Relic | Proposed history | Visible or audible detail to pursue | Flavour line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nailer of Small Mercies | A worker-housing rivet gun used on doors, bed frames and leaking roofs. Its casing records borrowed parts from several households. | One mismatched washer jumps with the recoil; its grip is polished by years of handling. | “For things too small to requisition a crew.” |
| Bell of the Last Shift | A workshop bell once announced that work could stop. Its clapper was repaired so often that the repair dates cover the original maker's mark. | A solid physical strike followed by an imperfect double resonance. | “The second note is the repair.” |
| Procession Gear | A guide gear from a slow service conveyor used to carry heavy parts between benches. Successive repairs gave it an uneven but dependable gait. | An irregular clatter and a visibly replaced tooth make its orbit individual. | “Still making the rounds.” |
| Candle-Nailer | A flare tool used by recovery crews to mark searched spaces and leave a light where someone was expected back. | Wax collected beneath the barrel; a smoke ribbon remains briefly after the shot. | “Leave a light where you have looked.” |
| Cable of Contrition | A broad maintenance line used to pull several warped fittings back into alignment. A past over-tightening earned it a workshop nickname. | A sweeping line under load, with strain spread visibly across its reach. | “The apology is in the replacement splice.” |
| Penance Winch | A telescoping service arm built to retrieve dropped equipment from inaccessible shafts. Its accepted extension limit has been amended in several hands. | Reach, latch and heavy reeling express a precise retrieval, distinct from Cable's broad sweep. | “The limit was written by someone with a shorter arm.” |
| Hymn Coil | A copper tuning unit used to bring distant drive systems into a shared rhythm. Local crews learned to recognise their section by its tone. | A tuning motion and clean, repeated pulses; an uneven starting hum settles as it operates. | “Third winding: waterworks. Fourth: unknown.” |
| Altar Mortar | A small chapel font served a community that washed grit from its hands before eating. Its heavy bowl was later mounted to a pressure launcher. | A chipped thumb rest survives on the lip; the base settles under each discharge. | “The bowl has survived several changes of purpose.” |
| Mercy Rail | The housing Nailer, rebuilt through the Saint's Rivet, now operates with the force of structural joining equipment. Its domestic repairs remain visible in the larger frame. | Bracing, preparation, decisive discharge and recoil recovery; a temporary clear line through the crowd makes its result legible. | “The little housing gun has taken on structural work.” |
| The Great Toll | The Bell is re-hung so its repaired voice can carry farther. It still has the recognisable second note. | The bell's movement visibly causes the radial event; allow the tail to decay without masking other attacks. | “They heard the repair from the next district.” |
| Foundry Censer, proposed | A perforated carrier once moved live embers between furnaces during difficult restarts. Its bearer was expected to keep walking. | A hanging mass, ember weight and uneven smoke should explain the source of any eventual effect. | “Carry the heat. Someone has let theirs go out.” |
| Welded Halo, proposed | A circular alignment jig held mismatched assemblies steady for field welding. Its lopsided welds record adaptations to bodies it was never sized for. | Clamps and local alignment marks distinguish it from a generic magical ring. Its overlap with Procession Gear still needs a mechanics review. | “Round enough for this body.” |

These histories preserve existing item names and broad identity. They do not add promised statuses, healing or new geometries to the current runtime. Foundry Censer and Welded Halo remain proposals from the merged expansion document. In particular, a lore reference to repair does not imply that firing a weapon repairs the player or an objective.

Catalysts should carry equally specific provenance. Saint's Rivet is a recovered structural fastener with a maker's witness mark beneath later repairs. Cracked Bell Clapper has a replaced striking face, fitted to keep the bell's familiar note. Black Candle is lit while friends decide which parts of an irreparable body should travel on. Quiet Gear was hand-fitted for machinery beside a sleeping room. These are proposed histories; their mechanical effects stay as documented in the runtime catalogue.

Plain recovery kits contain patch plates, sealant and a charged connector. Give their collection the sound and brief motion of a useful repair. Scrap is workable material. Relic Shards can be physically distinctive fragments of calibrated assemblies, valuable because their remaining tolerances permit a more ambitious rebuild. No new currency is needed.

**Blessings as living traditions**

Blessings should express what a community considers good work. They have apprentices, disagreements and daily practices. Their mechanical biases remain open to hybrid builds; selecting a doctrine does not assign the Saint a moral alignment.

| Tradition | Conviction and everyday practice | Tension that gives it character |
|---|---|---|
| Workshop Gospel | Keep useful things in service. Apprentices first learn to make a repair that someone else can undo. | Repair can become refusal to admit that an old purpose should end. |
| Bell Ward | A warning is a promise to neighbours. Signals are tested and taught to newcomers. | Shared timing can turn into enforced conformity. |
| The Procession | Nobody should have to carry a difficult load alone. Tools and journeys are organised around company. | Staying together can make it hard for an individual to leave. |
| The Mourner | Preserve what matters when a body cannot continue. Families choose which marks and parts are kept. | Memory can become a burden if everything must be carried forever. |
| Quiet Order | Listen before adding another command. Workshops reserve periods in which a struggling mechanism can be heard. | Restraint can become avoidance when action is needed. |
| Salvage Rite | A useful part deserves another chance. Exchanges record who rebuilt an object, not only who owned it. | Seeing value in everything can slide into treating neighbours as material. |

The proposed Gift layer can later draw from small bequests and workshop favours. It must not gate basic UI clarity or essential information. For example, the expansion proposal's Honest Scale should be ordinary shop behaviour, not an item the player must equip to understand a purchase.

| Place | What continues there | What the player notices while moving |
|---|---|---|
| Collapsed Workshop | A shared repair yard survives beneath the remains of an earlier building. Ada keeps benches open between dangerous work cycles. | Clean working patches amid soot, borrowed tools with coloured ties, a charging corner with mismatched sockets. |
| Brass Choir | Signal towers once coordinated public life. New settlements disagree over which schedule deserves to be heard. | Different bells answered from a distance; residential alterations on old signal platforms. |
| Rootworks | Pump lines and service tunnels now support a patchwork of local uses. | Pipes diverted around homes, damp mineral growth, small machines sheltering near warm valves. |
| Red Foundry | Furnaces support the valley's remaining heavy repairs, at considerable material cost. | Warm inhabited margins beside dangerous process floors; carefully saved offcuts. |
| Pale Archive | Service records preserve both useful knowledge and obsolete claims of ownership. | Handwritten corrections beside official plates; shelves reorganised for new bodies. |

Use the existing district names rather than adding factions for every place. Null Assembly can become the Engine's decommissioning service: an institution convinced that unsafe equipment must be taken out of service, including inhabited improvisations. That gives it a comprehensible position without needing an extra supernatural force.

Ada-9 should be a recurring practical relationship. She knows which part of the Saint she repaired and is irritated by how long it has lasted. Her shop is a portable bench opened during a lull; the paused transaction remains a gameplay convenience, with no lore requirement to explain frozen time.

Brother Coil maintains signals that once saved lives. His attachment to common timing has a reasonable origin. Morrow stores small sound recordings and maker plates at the request of families, and has firm opinions about the space unnecessary memorials occupy. Red-13 is a furnace bearer who judges plans by whether they can keep a settlement warm tonight. Their arguments become more interesting when each is good at something useful.

The Archivist Prime should know a great deal about the Saint's parts and still misunderstand the person assembled from them. The First Engine should make a serious case for coordination: shared power can protect everyone during a hard season. Neither needs to know the player's build and invent a bespoke counter to it.

**How the fiction supports the game**

A run is a dangerous journey through active work districts. The Saint draws scrutiny as unregistered moving equipment. Authored waves express work cycles and escalation in the district; they are not literal shifts in an endless punishment plot. Shops are intervals for rebuilding, and the boss blocks onward passage through its ongoing operation. Victory remains survival and boss completion.

Optional repairs create small signs of care: a lamp comes back on, a charging bench warms or a local machine can complete a task. The stated reward remains worthwhile in play, and the player can leave. No compassion meter punishes a build that skips repairs. A quiet environmental acknowledgement often delivers more flavour than another branching quest.

Treat an evolution as the moment the Saint finds a new use for its inherited parts. Keep the old wear marks. A new silhouette should look assembled from its former self, so the player can recognise the history of their run. Avoid claiming that every combination restores the machine's secretly intended true form.

Failure should be brief and respectful. A future return-to-bench vignette could show Ada recovering a damaged chassis; it must not promise retention of a lost build or require a resurrection economy. Saving and retrying need no elaborate metaphysical explanation.

The full campaign can retain four acts with a revised emotional progression:

| Act | External movement | Personal change |
|---|---|---|
| First Shift | Leave the workshop and reach the next inhabited district. | The Saint thinks being useful means following its surviving instruction. |
| Contested Districts | Learn why settlements need different things from the same infrastructure. | It discovers that good workers can disagree about what should be repaired. |
| Memory Works | Recover records of the body's successive repairs. | It abandons the search for one correct original self. |
| First Engine | Decide what relationship the valley should have with its old coordinator. | It accepts responsibility for a choice no task plate can make for it. |

Give the ending options real costs. Restoring coordination brings dependable shared services but concentrates authority. Repurposing the network gives settlements greater say but requires difficult cooperation and uneven service. Dismantling central control prevents its commands from dominating local life but sacrifices capabilities smaller systems cannot immediately replace. Avoid coding repair choices as automatic votes for obedience or making one ending the obviously virtuous answer.

| Subject | Grand tragic version | Broad comic version | Recommended voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saint's body | “A vessel for the sorrow of a dying god.” | “Several workplace violations in a coat.” | “That bracket was temporary.” |
| Nailer | “An instrument of mercy forged in the last war.” | “Percussive customer service.” | “For things too small to requisition a crew.” |
| Rust Pilgrim | “It wanders beneath a heaven that will not answer.” | “Your repair warranty has found you.” | “The route is obsolete. The repairs are excellent.” |
| Bell | “Its lament carries the names of the fallen.” | “Clocking out is now an area attack.” | “The second note is the repair.” |

The recommended voice works through practical detail and understated affection. Use the broader comic register occasionally in signs or an especially dry character. Reserve elevated language for rare moments of awe. An entire catalogue written as either a eulogy or a joke would lose range.

**A small playable expression of the direction**

This is a proposed presentation slice, not an expansion of the launch scope. Use the existing workshop, Saint, enemy roster and shop.

The opening view contains an old public notice explaining that the district was evacuated and a charging bench that has clearly been adapted since. Ada recognises her temporary bracket on the Saint. The surviving task plate gives the journey its immediate direction. Combat begins quickly; the player can ignore the environmental text.

During the first combat, the Rivet Hound's inspection pose makes its charge feel like a damaged work operation. The Nailer visibly recoils as an old household tool under strain. A field repair pickup produces a small patching response. Optional restoration lights a previously dark corner; the player can continue moving through the arena without doing it.

The first shop pairs each plain mechanical description with one optional flavour line. The new body attachment remains visible on the Saint. At the end of the site, Ada notices the machine's changed silhouette. A short exchange or visual beat acknowledges the journey; no lore quiz or mandatory codex interrupts the next run.

Evaluate that slice by asking a player what the Saint is, what the Hound was trying to do, and why the weapon looked the way it did. Also ask what they remember with affection. Correct answers to lore questions alone would not establish the desired tone.
