**Scrap Saint: weapon and item inspiration compendium**

Research date: 15 September 2026. Status: design reference; no new gameplay is implemented by this document.

The ambition is breadth of imagination. A good catalogue gives players different things to anticipate, different ways to move, and different reasons to care about a purchase. This compendium contains 45 reference entries across Slime 3K, Brotato and Vampire Survivors, including weapon families, transformations and supporting items. It is a curated selection, not an exhaustive inventory of their current releases or DLC.

Each entry separates a documented behaviour from our interpretation of its value. Linked developer announcements are primary evidence; wikis and guides are community evidence and can lag patches. No damage values, balance rankings, sales claims or claims of personal playtesting are inferred here. Some Vampire Survivors pages were accessible through indexed excerpts only. Slime 3K community descriptions include Early Access material; later patch notes take precedence where available.

The player's instruction governs how we use this research: do not copy the references. Names, objects, jokes, art, sound, exact attack choreography and recipes stay with their games. We can study general principles such as readable causality, changing attack coverage, meaningful opportunity costs and satisfying transformation. A renamed version of a reference weapon fails this brief.

**The perspective that guides the catalogue**

My design judgment is that each game is especially useful for a different question:

| Reference | Question it helps us ask | What deserves scrutiny |
|---|---|---|
| Slime 3K | Can I remember the object and what it does after one encounter? | A funny name can carry more personality than the actual attack. |
| Brotato | Can this purchase make an unusual build work? | Strong stat interactions can be difficult to see in combat. |
| Vampire Survivors | Does growing stronger visibly change my relationship with the crowd? | Sustained spectacle can erase the distinctions between weapons. |

These are lenses, not claims that each game possesses only one strength. The risks in the tables are design concerns to investigate, not measured player-review findings.

**Brotato: weapons with a job in the build**

Brotato is valuable here because a weapon can contribute to combat, recovery or purchasing power. Its ordinary-looking objects become interesting through their rules. The following entries use community documentation; exact scaling and current balance should be checked in a playable reference build before any detailed comparison.

| ID and reference | Documented behaviour | Principle worth studying | Design risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| B01 · [Pruner](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Pruner) | A thrusting weapon also spawns a garden that produces fruit. | A weapon can create something useful to collect, giving its owner a second kind of reward. | A stationary source can turn a roaming build into a camping routine. |
| B02 · [Wrench](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Wrench) | A sweeping weapon supplies a turret; its tier changes the turret type. | Upgrading can visibly alter the work performed by an auxiliary machine. | Damage attributed to an unseen helper may feel disconnected from the purchase. |
| B03 · [Thief Dagger](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Thief_Dagger) | Critical kills with this weapon have a chance to grant material. | Combat investment and economic investment can reinforce each other. | Kill ownership and chance can make the payoff hard to understand. |
| B04 · [Ghost Axe](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Ghost_Axe) | Weapon kills accumulate damage-stat gains retained after selling it during the run. | An early tool can leave a useful history after the build moves on. | Compounding rewards can make an early advantage decisive. |
| B05 · [Spiky Shield](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Spiky_Shield) | A thrusting shield weapon whose damage scales with armour. See also the [scaling explanation](https://teemo.dev/game-design/brotato/systems/stats-and-formulas-reference/). | A defensive investment can support an offensive identity. | A double-benefit stat can become the obvious purchase every time. |
| B06 · [Potato Thrower](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Potato_Thrower) | A ranged support weapon with substantial knockback; [maximum health contributes to its scaling](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Max_HP). | The same survivability investment can support a different delivery method and preferred distance. | A quirky object alone cannot distinguish otherwise similar stat conversions. |
| B07 · [Cacti Club](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Cacti_Club) | Melee hits spawn additional projectiles. | Contact can begin a second, visibly connected action. | Secondary effects can overwhelm the initiating impact. |
| B08 · [Lightning Shiv](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Lightning_Shiv) | A melee hit creates a lightning projectile aimed at another enemy; higher tiers add bounces. | One weapon can connect close combat with distant consequences. | If the second event is hard to follow, the build feels like unrelated damage. |
| B09 · [Shuriken](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shuriken) | Critical hits enable projectile bounces. | A probability stat can change attack coverage in a visible way. | Low investment may hide the weapon's advertised identity. |
| B10 · [Plank](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Plank) | Melee hits can cause explosions. | An ordinary object can exceed expectations through a surprising consequence. | A generic explosion may become the answer for too many weapons. |
| B11 · [Jousting Lance](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Jousting_Lance) | Grants movement speed and carries a damage penalty while stationary. | Equipment can express a preferred movement habit. | A movement rule can become a chore if the player cannot feel its benefit. |

My strongest Brotato lessons are B01, B03, B07 and B09. They broaden what a weapon contributes: create a resource, finance a build, start a second action, or change coverage through investment. B05 is strategically rich but needs especially clear feedback; an invisible armour coefficient does little for the sense of wielding something memorable.

**Vampire Survivors: weapons that change the space and rhythm of a run**

This selection focuses on established base-game weapon lines. Several entries include evolutions or unions because the contrast between the original and transformed behaviour is the useful reference. It deliberately leaves the large crossover catalogue outside this research scope.

| ID and reference | Documented behaviour | Principle worth studying | Design risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| V01 · [Knife](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Knife) | Fires in the direction the character faces. | Movement and orientation can provide agency within automatic combat. | Escaping a threat may point the attack away from the intended target. |
| V02 · [Garlic](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Garlic) | A close aura damages enemies and makes them more susceptible to knockback and freezing. | A modest attack can change confidence and support other weapons. | Its early usefulness may obscure a later change in role. |
| V03 · [Soul Eater](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Eater) | Garlic's evolution adds healing opportunities and gains damage through recovered health. | A transformation can connect survival with offensive growth. | The useful change can disappear inside an unobtrusive stat counter. |
| V04 · [King Bible](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/King_Bible) → [Unholy Vespers](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Unholy_Vespers) | An orbiting attack becomes a ring whose default duration and cooldown allow continuous coverage. | Removing downtime can be a perceptible transformation even when the broad geometry survives. | Continuous coverage can make positioning less consequential. |
| V05 · [Santa Water](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Weapons) → [La Borra](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/La_Borra) | Damaging ground zones evolve into zones that approach the player and grow. | A transformation can change the relationship between movement and an existing effect. | Overlapping fields can lose their individual identity and obscure the floor. |
| V06 · [Runetracer](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Runetracer) | Projectiles pass through enemies and bounce around. | A persistent trajectory lets the player anticipate an attack's future usefulness. | Off-screen results and dense trails can make contribution hard to judge. |
| V07 · [Song of Mana](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Song_of_Mana) | A piercing attack occupies a vertical column. | A strong directional shape can make lateral repositioning meaningful. | A directional specialty can become frustrating when coverage is too unreliable. |
| V08 · [Clock Lancet](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Clock_Lancet) | A non-damaging freezing beam advances through directions clockwise. | A combat slot can create opportunities to move instead of directly killing. | A damage-only comparison undervalues the tool; effects need visible outcomes. |
| V09 · [Laurel](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Laurel) | A rechargeable shield absorbs hits and briefly grants invulnerability. | A limited buffer can buy recovery from a movement mistake. | Excessive uptime can erase the reason to dodge. |
| V10 · [Shadow Pinion](https://vgkami.com/vampire-survivors-how-to-unlock-shadow-pinion-and-what-it-does/) | Movement leaves drills; stopping launches them toward the faced direction. | An existing input can become a deliberate attack command. | An unexplained stop-to-fire rule can feel unresponsive. This is not a proposal to make stopping compulsory in Scrap Saint. |
| V11 · [Gatti Amari](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Gatti_Amari) | Cats can fight each other, interact with food and harm the player. | A summon can have behaviour the player forms stories about. | Autonomy becomes irritation when costs or outcomes feel arbitrary. |
| V12 · [Phieraggi](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Phieraggi) | Two firearm weapons unite into an attack that scales with available revivals; [its lasers rotate around the player](https://progameguides.com/vampire-survivors/how-to-evolve-and-unite-the-phiera-der-tuphello-and-eight-the-sparrow-weapons-in-vampire-survivors/). | An investment can change both the attack's form and the meaning of another resource. | The unusual dependency can require explanation outside combat. |
| V13 · [Peachone + Ebony Wings → Vandalier](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Vandalier) | The union bombards two circulating zones and frees a weapon slot. | Transformation can reward commitment with room for a new choice. | A long setup can feel like paying a tax before the fun begins. |
| V14 · [Pentagram → Gorgeous Moon](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Gorgeous_Moon) | The evolution replaces pickup-destroying erasure with a staged attack that creates extra experience and collects gems. | A payoff can resolve a previous drawback through a recognisable sequence. | Deleting earned rewards is costly frustration; the lesson is the staged payoff, not that penalty. |
| V15 · [Axe](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2938626360) → [Death Spiral](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Death_Spiral) | Arcing throws become outward-firing scythes with extensive penetration. | A transformation can expand the directions the player can pressure. | If every evolution becomes universal coverage, the catalogue converges. |

My strongest Vampire Survivors lessons are V05, V08, V10 and V14. They concern moving effects, non-damage value, input expression and staged anticipation. V13 adds a different reward: the build itself gains room to change. We should preserve a weapon's recognisable action through an evolution even when its usefulness expands.

**Supporting items: the connections between weapons matter too**

These are separate from the weapon catalogue. Their value comes from changing what a build can do with things it already owns. The observations are sourced; the proposed lessons and risks are our analysis.

| ID and reference | Documented behaviour | Principle worth studying | Design risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| I01 · Brotato [Bandana](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Bandana) | Adds projectile penetration with a damage penalty. | A support purchase can trade strength per hit for coverage. | The shop must show which owned effects actually benefit. |
| I02 · Brotato [Ricochet](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Ricochet) | Adds a bounce with a damage penalty. | A modifier can change target distribution across several attacks. | Interaction order and damage falloff can hide the real tradeoff. |

For our relic shop, the important lesson is to show the consequence for the current machine. A card should explain the affected owned weapons and preview a visible change. The compendium does not propose reinstating repair services in shop slots.

**How to turn the references into original work**

Start each new design from Scrap Saint's world. Ask what the machine once did for people, what has broken, and what the Saint mistakenly or earnestly believes its purpose to be. Derive its physical action from that history. Consult the references afterward to test clarity, expression and variety.

Our material includes worn industrial components, improvised repairs, maintenance rituals and machines carrying memories of useful work. A source object reskinned with brass or given a devotional name is still too close to the source.

Use this sequence for each candidate:

1. Write its former practical purpose and the player's desired feeling without mentioning another game's weapon.
2. Sketch its physical operation, including preparation, contact and recovery. Identify where force goes and what visibly changes.
3. State the combat action in a plain sentence. The base form must express its identity before an evolution or rare combination.
4. Describe the movement choices it permits. Include how it behaves during retreat and against sparse enemies.
5. Use two or more reference principles to critique the draft. Combine lessons about player experience; do not combine borrowed objects into a collage.
6. Compare the draft against both the references and our own roster. Rework any recognisable object/action/choreography match, or any overlap that adds no new player experience.
7. Decide whether the idea deserves a weapon slot, a catalyst, a Blessing interaction or a presentation improvement. More weapons are only one way to add variety.

This process is a creative discipline, not a claim that a checklist can prove universal originality. Reference names stay in research notes and never become implementation placeholders for new content.

**A framework for judging the next design**

Use these questions as a profile. Do not total them into a score that allows visual quality to compensate for a weak action, or complexity to compensate for poor readability.

| Dimension | Question | Evidence to seek in a prototype |
|---|---|---|
| Object and purpose | Why would this relic exist in our world? | A silhouette and one sentence imply its former use. |
| Physical action | What satisfying thing happens when it operates? | Preparation, force, contact and recovery read without explanatory text. |
| Space | What area, distance or relationship does it influence? | Players can anticipate its useful position. |
| Rhythm | What do I look forward to happening next? | A sound or motion communicates its cycle and payoff. |
| Movement agency | How can I help it work while remaining free to roam? | A player can name an optional positioning choice after a short use. |
| Build relationships | Why might a different purchase become interesting? | A visible interaction works with several compatible builds. |
| Transformation | What meaningful change does its evolution deliver? | The before/after difference is identifiable at ordinary gameplay zoom. |
| Contribution | Can I tell what this relic accomplished? | The effect connects to a hit, displacement, resource or other result. |
| Breadth | What experience would disappear if this entry were removed? | Removing it leaves a clear gap in the catalogue. |
| Distinctiveness | Does its identity arise from Scrap Saint? | It survives comparison without relying on a renamed reference concept. |

The intended payoff might be a satisfying repeated action, relief, anticipation, mischief, or the sense of a machine doing useful work. It does not have to be a larger damage number. A simple weapon with a strong physical action can earn its place alongside a complex build interaction.

**Where to look for breadth in Scrap Saint**

These are questions for original design exploration, not approved features or a request to add every row. They should be considered against the current authored weapon proposals before inventing more content.

| Area to explore | Prompt rooted in our game | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanical force | What does tension, compression or release look and sound like at gameplay scale? | Keep preparation and contact legible; avoid repeating the same beam payoff. |
| Timing | Can a machine's operating cycle create anticipation through motion and sound? | Its base form must remain useful without a specific catalyst. |
| Useful remnants | Can evidence of completed work have a brief, understandable consequence? | Avoid runaway chains and persistent floor clutter. |
| Mobile assistance | What useful work could a companion perform while travelling with the Saint? | It must not introduce mandatory stationary defence. |
| Spatial relationships | Can distance, alignment or changing position alter how a mechanism operates? | Reward choices without requiring weapon-specific enemies or arenas. |
| Recovery expression | How can a dropped repair item feel like restoring a physical machine? | Preserve field recovery and optional repairs; keep healing services out of shop slots. |
| Build transformation | Can an evolution alter a mechanism's purpose while preserving its history? | Show the changed operation and its consequences, not just a new title. |
| Gentle absurdity | Where can earnest maintenance ritual produce a surprising result? | Humour should come from this world's people and machines, not borrowed food or pop-culture jokes. |

For the current build, improving the distinction between Penance Winch and Cable is more useful than adding another pull effect. Their target selection, physical motion and enemy response should make their different roles apparent. Mercy Rail should communicate preparation, discharge and aftermath clearly against the ordinary roster. It remains optional; encounters must not be tailored around its ownership.

The main mode remains free movement with optional repairs. No proposal here restores relay defence, manual boss-interrupt requirements or encounters that demand a particular weapon. Older planning documents contain superseded versions of those ideas; the user's later direction and the current runtime notes govern this compendium.
